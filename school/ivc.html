Indus Valley Civilization

Introduction

The Indus Valley Civilization was one of the world's earliest urban civilizations, flourishing around 2500 BCE in what is now Pakistan and northwest India. It was known for its advanced urban planning, sophisticated drainage systems, and impressive architecture. The civilization is also referred to as the Harappan Civilization, named after one of its major sites, Harappa.

Key Features of the Civilization

Urban Planning: The cities of the Indus Valley, such as Mohenjo-Daro and Harappa, were built using a grid layout with well-organized streets and brick houses.
Drainage System: The civilization had a highly efficient drainage system, with covered drains running along the streets, ensuring cleanliness and hygiene.
Trade and Economy: The people engaged in trade with distant regions, including Mesopotamia, exchanging goods like beads, pottery, and textiles.
Writing System: The Indus script remains undeciphered to this day, but it suggests a form of communication and record-keeping.
Art and Craft: The civilization is famous for its terracotta figurines, jewelry, seals, and pottery, which reflect their artistic and cultural achievements.
